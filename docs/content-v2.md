Status: 2026-10-31. Approved by Max: red lines R1–R10 (R9 reworded), institutions/civic-culture statements I1–I7 with their keys, the CF review's separator statements, and a critical-rationalist (Popperian) liberalism archetype. Calls marked **[my call]** were made by the content agent and can be reversed.

## 1. Where the new statements go

- **Red lines are off-axis** (`content/items/red.json`, ids `red-01`…`red-10`). They are asked at every length, after the axis statements, in both frames, on the normal grid. They are never scored: not on an axis, not in the careless index, Reality Gap, coverage or answered/total counts. They act only through commitments (§3).
- **Every other new statement is an axis item in the Full tier only** **[my call]**. Quick (4 per axis) and Standard (6 per axis) are unchanged, so their calibration, run history, extend-run and share links keep their meaning. Full grows from 120 to 144 axis statements (10–16 per axis: fed 12, dem 16, lib 12, col 12, pla 14, glo 10, int 10, mil 12, mul 12, rel 12, pro 10, tec 12). With the red lines, the lengths are 58 / 82 / 154 statements.
  - Why not replace weaker Standard items: that changes what existing Standard runs mean and breaks nesting for extend-run, for a gain only in Standard. The cost is that the separators work only at Full; Standard still cannot separate the pairs they target unless a commitment does.
  - Why not an unscored "separator" set (the review's suggestion): an unscored statement affects results only through a commitment, so most separators would cost the respondent time for nothing. On an axis they also move distance a little.
- **Key balance is kept per axis** ("both keyings"): every axis adds statements in +/− pairs, conditionals are balanced across keys, and facets are balanced across keys. Acquiescence invariance (all-agree → 0) holds at every length.

### Red-line notes and the red-10 rewording (2026-11-01)

**red-10 reworded** from "Using violence against political opponents is acceptable if they threaten the nation." to "Violence against peaceful political opponents is acceptable if they are seen as a threat to the nation." (Max's feedback). The old wording mixed two things: violence against people for their politics, and force against a genuine threat (someone planning an attack, an armed takeover). Liberal and libertarian traditions accept the second as legitimate defence of rights; classical liberalism's own commitment says "force is only for defending rights against actual aggression". So a respondent who read "threaten the nation" as a genuine threat and agreed answered decisively against 25 must-disagree red-line commitments (critical-rationalism, classical-liberalism, minarchism and most democratic ideologies) and was **falsely ruled out of all 25**. "Peaceful" puts the line at violence for someone's politics; "seen as a threat" keeps the move being tested, labelling peaceful opposition a threat to the nation.
- All 26 red-10 commitments were re-checked against the new wording and note: the 25 must-disagree entries hold (more clearly than before); fascism's must-agree still holds (squadrismo and the Matteotti murder were violence against peaceful opponents framed as enemies of the nation). Fascism's `why` was edited to say so ("opponents who weaken it are enemies even when peaceful, and squad violence against them was our founding method"); the other `why` texts already fit. None dropped or narrowed. `commitments-raw.json` is the raw lens output and is not re-read by the merge (it reads the lens directory), so it keeps the original text as a record.
- The coded sheet values for red-10 (and `items-for-coders.txt`, the packet the coders saw) are from the old wording and were not re-coded. The change only narrows what agreeing means, so humane-side codes can only firm up; every committed ideology is already at −3/−3 and fascism at +3/+3. Re-coding red-10 with the three coders is left for the next coding pass.
- `just separation` is unchanged (byte-identical JSON before and after: Quick 93, Standard 68, Full 42 unseparated; 605 / 623 / 804 pairs ruled apart by a commitment), since it reads the sheets and commitments, not wording. `just commitments-audit`: 0 commitments disagree with the sheets, none over 2%. Red lines don't affect the fitted axis thresholds; calibration's end-to-end reports simulate red-line answers only for their commitment effects, which don't depend on wording.
- **Saved links keep working:** item ids and the red-line order are unchanged, and share links store red-line answers by position (codes 0–3), so an old link decodes as before. It does not preserve meaning: a red-10 answer given before the change shows beside the new wording and is checked against the commitments as if given to it. Local runs and sessions are migrated on load (src/state/wording.ts, `RED_WORDING = 2`): an old *agree* answer to red-10 is dropped frame by frame, because agreeing with the broad wording doesn't imply agreeing with the narrow one, while disagree and neutral answers are kept, because they still hold. A statement left with neither frame shows as not answered. Shared links still carry the rule-outs computed when they were made; versioning red-line wording in the payload is left as a follow-up.

The notes are part of the provenance gate now: `just overlap` checks notes (axis and red) as well as statements.

## 4. Pair separation

Breakpoint (CF review §5): a pair is separated at a length if some statement asked at that length has one ideology at ≥ +2 and the other at ≤ −2 (median sheets, either frame). `just separation` (`scripts/separation.ts`) prints the table.

| after (48 ideologies, 1,128 pairs) | 93 | 68 | 42 |
| after, original 1,081 pairs only | | | 38 |
| pairs ruled apart by a commitment (before → after) | 571 → 605 | 591 → 623 | 672 → 804 |

Quick and Standard rise only because the new archetype adds pairs (its Full-only defining statements aren't asked there).

Newly separated: anarcho-communism ~ anarcho-syndicalism, democratic socialism ~ Trotskyism, classical liberalism ~ Georgism, civic nationalism ~ Kemalism, left populism ~ libertarian municipalism. Minarchism ~ anarcho-capitalism now splits on state existence (`fed-11` +3 vs −3, with must-agree / must-disagree commitments on both sides) rather than only on the `dem-01` artefact.

Still unseparated at Full, and why:
- **Centre and centre-left liberal neighbours** (progressive / social liberalism, social democracy, centrism, third way, constitutional monarchism, communitarianism, liberal conservatism, neoliberalism, and critical-rationalist liberalism against social liberalism, centrism, third way and neoliberalism). The coders put these at soft, same-signed positions on nearly everything, including the new welfare, union, monarchy and piecemeal statements (e.g. constitutional monarchism −3 vs centrism +1 on an elected head of state; social democracy +3 vs progressive liberalism +2 on sectoral bargaining). Their differences are of degree, which this breakpoint deliberately ignores. Constitutional monarchism is now at least ruled out by its own commitment for anyone firmly for an elected head of state.
- **Classical liberalism ~ minarchism / neoliberalism**: classical liberalism is coded as only mildly accepting public provision (col-11 +1 vs minarchism +3); the tradition is divided on it, so no firm split is honest.
- **Libertarian municipalism ~ anarcho-communism / anarcho-syndicalism**, **eco-socialism ~ democratic socialism / green politics**, **national conservatism ~ traditional conservatism / right populism**, **communitarianism ~ Islamic democracy / Christian democracy / distributism**, **Islamic democracy ~ distributism / Peronism**, **African socialism ~ left populism**, **transhumanism ~ social / progressive liberalism**: the differences are of emphasis or strategy; each side is at most mildly against the other's defining statement.

Further separators would need statements that one side firmly rejects, which for these pairs mostly don't exist; merging or disclosing ("this test can't tell A from B") is the honest alternative and is left to the fit-set headline work.
