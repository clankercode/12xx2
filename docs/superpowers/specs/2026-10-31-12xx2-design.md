# 12 Axes² — Design (v2)

Status: design v2, 2026-10-31. v2 incorporates a four-lens adversarial review of v1 (builder, psychometrics, political neutrality, UX/virality/privacy). Of its 62 findings, the key ones were verified by seeded simulation. Inputs: `docs/research/` (01–09 plus critic addenda). Calls marked **[my call]** are the agent's decisions; Max can reverse any of them.

## 1. Intent

12 Axes² ("twelve axes squared", slug `12xx2`) is a political values quiz. It keeps the 12-axis model of the original MIT-licensed 12 Axes test but **answers every statement twice on one grid**:

- **Ideal system**: your own ideal, already up and running.
- **Here and now**: the country whose laws you mainly live under, as it is today.

**Why.** Single-frame quizzes conflate *what is just* with *what a situation requires*. Take a Dane and an Iranian with identical values. On redistribution they score differently, because Iran's problems can't be fixed within its legal framework and Denmark's mostly can. 12 Axes² measures the two separately and reports how they differ.

**The ideal is ideology-relative, so the relationship is non-linear.** A theocrat, a socialist and an anarcho-capitalist each imagine a different working system, so "Now minus Ideal" means different things for different people. The design handles this four ways:
- Both frames are scored on the same scale.
- Profiles are matched against archetypes that themselves have an ideal *and* a transitional programme.
- Respondents can optionally place their perceived status quo, so each gap can be typed relative to *their own* ideal (§6.5).
- Reason chips let respondents say *why* their answers differ.

### Goals
1. **Accurate and defensible.**
   - Every displayed number has a published formula, an uncertainty band or baseline, and thresholds that are calibrated by committed, seeded simulation rather than hand-tuned.
   - Every label is gated against noise.
   - It fixes the known flaws of both upstream quizzes (`docs/criticisms.md`).
2. **Fun and shareable.**
   - A compact identity: "Ideal: A · Here and now: B", or "Constant: A".
   - A striking 12×2 dumbbell visual.
   - Share cards, rich link previews, and a compare-with-a-friend loop.
3. **Private by construction.**
   - Static SPA; everything is computed on the device.
   - No analytics and no third-party requests.
   - Scores travel only in a URL `#fragment`, and only when the user shares.

### Non-goals
No backend, accounts, telemetry, ads or paywall. No matching to living people, parties or countries. No population percentiles. Not a validated instrument; we say so.

### Anti-goals
- Labels that are mostly noise.
- Pass treated as neutral.
- Gaps framed as hypocrisy or inconsistency.
- Loaded pole names.
- Any text or vectors from 12axes.vercel.app (all rights reserved).
- A noisy respondent labelled with an extremist ideology on a share card.

## 2. Content model

### 2.1 Axes **[my call: the 12 MIT concepts, each tightened to one construct; adherent-acceptable pole names; 4 groups × 3]**

Scores run −100…+100, with **negative = left pole**. MIT names are kept in a crosswalk on the methodology page, along with the vercel names.

| Group | id | Axis title | − pole | + pole | MIT names | Construct (sub-facet tags) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Governance | `fed` | Power distribution | Decentralized | Centralized | Federal/Unitary | Where authority sits: dispersed to regions and communities vs concentrated at the centre |
| Governance | `dem` | Decision-making | Democratic | Hierarchical | Democratic/Autocratic | Broad participation and contestable power vs decisions reserved to a few (monarch, council, party, experts, clergy) |
| Governance | `lib` | Liberty vs security | Liberty | Security | Freedom/Security | When they conflict: individual freedoms vs collective safety, order and policing. Owns all *coercion/enforcement* questions |
| Economy | `col` | Ownership | Collective | Private | Collectivize/Privatize | Ownership of productive assets (`ownership`), plus distribution of wealth (`distribution`). Bundled because they correlate empirically; disclosed |
| Economy | `pla` | Coordination | Planned | Market | Planned/Markets | Deliberate allocation vs prices and competition, whoever owns |
| Economy | `glo` | Trade | Open | Protected | Globalism/Protectionism | Openness of trade, capital and supply chains |
| World | `int` | Sovereignty | Internationalist | Sovereigntist | Internationalism/Nationalism | Pooled supranational authority and allegiance vs national primacy. **Sovereignty only** |
| World | `mil` | Use of force | Hawkish | Dovish | Militarist/Pacifist | Willingness to use force abroad (`use`), plus building military capacity (`capacity`) |
| World | `mul` | Newcomers | Pluralist | Assimilationist | Multicultural/Assimilationist | Openness to immigration (`volume`) and plural identities vs a shared common culture (`integration`). Bundled; disclosed |
| Culture | `rel` | Religion & state | Secular | Religious | Irreligious/Religious | Role of religion in **law and public institutions**, not personal faith |
| Culture | `pro` | Social norms | Progressive | Traditional | Progressive/Traditional | **Content**, not rate of change: permissive/egalitarian vs traditional norms on family, sexuality, gender and custom that society and its institutions should affirm. Not criminal enforcement (that is `lib`) |
| Culture | `tec` | Technology | Transhumanist | Bioconservative | Technology/Biology | Embracing vs restraining transformative technology (enhancement, germline editing, advanced AI, life extension) |

Axes are presented as **correlated facets**, not 12 independent dimensions. The methodology page says so, and lists the bundled sub-facets and the known gaps in the 12-axis model:
- no axis for remedies for historical injustice;
- no axis for emergency powers;
- immigration volume is bundled with integration.

### 2.2 Frames (respondent-facing text, fixed)

Both frames rate **the same statement**. The question is **agentless**: it names no state, system or ruler as the actor.

- **Ideal system**: "Picture your ideal system, already up and running and legitimate by your own lights. Its people are ordinary, fallible humans. That includes whoever holds power, whether that's assemblies, councils, a monarch, or no one. It still meets hard situations: downturns, conflict, crime, disruptive change. Other countries are as they realistically could be. **In your ideal system, how much do you agree?**"
- **Here and now**: "The country whose laws you mainly live under, as it is today: its real institutions, people and problems. Judge it as it exists, whatever you think its borders or legitimacy should be. **Here and now, how much do you agree?**"
- **Shared rule** (shown as a tip): "If a statement describes a situation, assume it's happening in the frame you're judging. Answer each frame on its own terms. Same answers and different answers are both fine."

Calls:
- **Hypothetical reading in both frames** [my call]. The "doesn't apply here" view goes into a reason chip.
- **The rest of the world is realistic, not ideal** [my call]. This is disclosed. Archetypes are coded under the identical stipulation (§2.6), so global-ideal archetypes remain reachable.
- **Fallibility, not personal rule, is what's stipulated.** Max's "no philosopher-king needed" becomes "whoever holds power is fallible". This keeps monarchist and other hierarchical ideals expressible. It is disclosed.

### 2.3 Items

- **Bank:** 12 axes × 10 = **120 items**, 5 keyed toward each pole per axis (`key: −1 | +1`).
- **Item fields:** `id`, `axis`, `key`, `facet`, `form` (`rule` | `conditional`), `level` (2 = clear, 3 = strong), `text`, optional `note` (defines a term). Wording is original.
- **Writing rules:** each rule is a data-validation check where it can be machine-checked, and an editorial review checklist item otherwise.
  1. Every statement is a **standing rule** ("Religious leaders should have a formal role in making law") or a **conditional** ("If a few people come to own most of the wealth, it should be redistributed").
  2. No status-quo-relative wording: "more than now", "too much", "currently", "still".
  3. No negations where an affirmative polar opposite exists. Not double-barrelled.
  4. No country-specific institutions or events.
  5. **Agent-neutral** unless the axis *is* about the agent. Economic outcomes must be answerable by anarchists.
  6. Polar items only, with extremity matched across the two keys of an axis: no "both camps agree" items and no level-4 caricatures.
  7. **Form parity:** on each axis, conditional items are split evenly between keys, and the conditions are written to favour each pole. For example, "If officials misuse surveillance powers…" favours Liberty.
  8. **Facet parity:** facet tags are balanced across keys.
  9. `rel` items speak of "religion" or "religious leaders" generally, never a named faith.
  10. Must read naturally in both frames under the agentless question.
  11. Reading level around grade 8; at most about 25 words.
- **Provenance gate:** `just overlap` compares every item with both upstream banks. They are stored only as salted hashes of 3-word shingles (the vercel text is never committed). An item fails at containment ≥ 0.5 of its stopword-stripped 3-gram shingles, or at any 6+ word exact run. The check runs in CI and fails closed.
- **Blind keying audit:** a reviewer from a different model family labels each item's pole without seeing its key. Any disagreement forces a rewrite.
- **Lengths:** nested and fixed. There is no random selection, so results are comparable across people. **Default: Standard.**

  | Mode | Items/axis | Total | Keying | Est. time | Hero |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Quick | 4 | 48 | 2:2 | ~7 min | full result, marked *low precision* |
  | Standard | 6 | 72 | 3:3 | ~11 min | full result |
  | Full | 10 | 120 | 5:5 | ~18 min | full result |

  Quick ⊂ Standard ⊂ Full. The order is a fixed, deterministic interleave: axes round-robin, keys alternating, and no two consecutive items on the same axis.

### 2.4 After-items context (optional, ~1.5 min) **[my call: moved after the items to avoid priming]**

1. **System Confidence (SC).** Six Here-and-now items on how well the system *functions* by its own rules. They are ideology-neutral: there is no elections or consent item. Keying is balanced 3:3, and each item is single-barrelled. Example drafts:
   - (+) "Rules here are applied to powerful people the same way as to everyone else."
   - (−) "Here, people with the right connections can get around the rules."
   - (+) "Officials here mostly do their jobs honestly."
   - (−) "Here, getting things done often takes a bribe."
   - (+) "When something goes seriously wrong here, it can be fixed through the system's own channels."
   - (−) "Here, those in charge are protected from consequences when they abuse their power."

   SC is the balanced keyed mean on a 0–100 scale, with a band. It needs at least 4 answers, including both keys. SC is never used in axis scoring.
2. **Country (optional).** Keyed on ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 and displayed with `Intl.DisplayNames` (neutral CLDR names, offline); the naming policy is disclosed. On the **results page only**, it shows World Bank WGI Rule of Law and Control of Corruption percentile ranks (CC BY 4.0, with their 90% bounds). They are labelled "external reference, as rated by the World Bank WGI", beside the SC the user already gave. WGI is never shown before SC is answered.

### 2.5 Status-quo sliders (optional)

"Where is your country now, relative to your answers?" Each axis track already shows the user's computed ◆ Ideal and ● Now. The slider has **no thumb until touched**, and an untouched slider counts as skipped.
- Because Q is placed relative to the user's own markers, it is commensurate with them. The typology uses only the relative order.
- The page discloses that Q is a direct placement, while I and N are item-derived.
- One tap skips the whole step.

### 2.6 Ideology archetypes

- **Catalogue:** about 44 archetypes.
- **Composition rule:** at least 3 archetypes in each family:
  - socialist/communist
  - social-democratic/liberal-left
  - liberal/centrist
  - conservative/traditional
  - religious
  - nationalist/populist
  - libertarian/market
  - anarchist/autonomist
  - hierarchical/monarchist/technocratic
- The catalogue includes non-Western traditions and 1–2 centrist or moderate archetypes.
- **No living individuals, parties or countries.** Names are neutral and self-descriptive.
- **Sensitive archetypes**, a symmetric policy: Fascism (historical) and Marxism–Leninism (Stalinist variant) are included as a matched pair. Theocratic absolutism is included. National Socialism, Khmer Rouge-style and jihadist ideologies are not. Sensitive archetypes can appear in lists, but fit (and so appear in the headline) only within the "very close" cut, and appear on cards only when the user opts in on the share sheet. Within "loosely" but not "very close" they are shown as "also close: X", never named.
- **Symmetric coding [replaces v1's direct vector coding]:**
  - Each coder *answers every item in both frames, as a sincere adherent would*. Coders get the exact respondent-facing frame text, the item bank, and a source packet with at least one primary source by adherents.
  - The engine scores those answer sheets. Archetype vectors are therefore **on exactly the user metric**.
  - Coders: **3 coders from at least 2 model families**, blind to each other. The published answer is the per-item median, which is then scored.
  - Published per archetype: per-axis spread (the range of the coders' scored vectors), agreement (ICC or mean absolute difference), and the coder model families.
  - Any axis with spread > 35 is reviewed: reconciled with a cited rationale, or weighted 0.5.
- **Archetype fields:**
  - `ideal[12]` and `now[12]` (both scored from sheets; Now uses the reference context "a present-day developed liberal democracy");
  - `weight[12]` ∈ {0.5, 1, 1.5}, normalised to Σw = 12 per archetype so that low weights can't act as attractors;
  - `family`, `sensitive`;
  - contrastive `summary` ("prioritises X over Y"), `signature` (Ideal/Now pattern) and `sources[]`.
- **Validation:** leave-one-coder-out. Each coder's own sheet must have its archetype in the top 3 of the catalogue built from the other two coders, for at least 85% of archetypes. Failures are reviewed.

## 3. Answer UX

### 3.1 The grid (default at all widths ≥ 340 px)
- A 7×7 grid. **x = Ideal** (columns, disagree → agree, left to right); **y = Here and now** (rows, agree at the top).
- Cell size is `clamp(38px, (100vw − 64px)/7, 64px)`. Tick labels are short (SD · D · d · N · a · A · SA), with full names in onboarding and on hover/long-press.
- The diagonal ("same in both") is subtly highlighted.
- Corner hints are purely positional and small: "Agree in both", "Disagree in both", "Agree in ideal only", "Agree here only".
- There is no preselected cell.
- Keyboard:
  - arrows move and Enter answers;
  - `S` = same as ideal;
  - `P` = pass both;
  - `I` / `N` = toggle pass for one frame;
  - Backspace = back.
- **Advance rule:** if |Δ| ≤ 2, it auto-advances after ~250 ms. If |Δ| ≥ 3, it stays: the reason-chip row appears with a focused **Next** button. Tapping a chip records it and advances. Chips are single-select, optional, and editable via Back.

### 3.2 Two-row mode (accessibility/preference toggle; automatic only below 340 px)
- Row 1 is Ideal and row 2 is Now, each with 7 radio pills. A "Same as ideal" button appears once row 1 is answered.
- The input mode is recorded per item. The share payload's flags record the dominant mode, and the calibration covers both modes' plausible copy rates.

### 3.3 Pass (Max's requirement)
Pass means "don't understand / don't know / won't answer". It is **missing data, never neutral**.
- "Pass" is a prominent button under the grid. "Unsure about Ideal" and "Unsure about Here & now" toggles collapse the grid to a single 7-option strip for the other frame.
- Coverage is reported ("you passed 7 of 72"). Axes with too little data say so.

### 3.4 Reason chips
Chips:
- crisis here
- conditions here aren't right
- can't trust those in charge
- fixing past wrongs
- a step toward the goal
- others would abuse it
- people here wouldn't accept it
- doesn't fit our culture or traditions
- can't afford it here
- not the state's role here
- doesn't apply here
- other

They are aggregated into "Your reasons".

### 3.5 Flow and persistence
1. Landing.
2. Mode select, with times.
3. One onboarding card plus a scored-nowhere practice item that teaches the grid. Before the first item, a one-line notice: "Progress is saved on this device. [Don't save]".
4. Items: progress, Back, resume.
5. Context (SC + country): optional, skippable.
6. Status-quo sliders: optional.
7. Results.

`localStorage` is wrapped in try/catch. "Clear my data" sits on the results page and in the footer.

## 4. Scoring

Notation:
- answers `x ∈ {−3…+3}` or `null`;
- keyed value `y = k·x`;
- for a set of items on axis a, let `m₊` / `m₋` be the means of `y` over answered items whose key is +1 / −1;
- `K = 100/3`.

### 4.1 Axis score (balanced form)
`S_f(a) = K · (m₊ + m₋)/2`.
- Equal item weights. Averaging the two key-direction means keeps acquiescence invariance (all-agree → 0) under **any** pass pattern. This is an invariant test, run with random pass masks.

### 4.2 Band
`SE = K · ½ · sqrt(SE₊² + SE₋²)`, with `SE_± = sqrt((s_±² + s0²)/n_±)` and `s0 = 1` scale point. It is clamped to 100.
- The methodology page describes this accurately: a within-person standard error with a floor, where ±1 SE ≈ 68%.
- Intensity labels are based on |S|: < 20 "centre"; 20–50 "leans"; 50–80 "strong"; ≥ 80 "very strong". A non-centre label appears only when the band excludes 0. Otherwise the axis is labelled "centre-ish (uncertain)".

### 4.3 Coverage
An axis/frame is scored if `n ≥ 3` **and** `n₊, n₋ ≥ 1`. Otherwise it shows "not enough answers" and is masked from matching.

### 4.4 Reality Gap and split rate (descriptive)
Over items answered in both frames:
- `RG = 100 · mean|Δ|/6`, where `Δ = x_N − x_I`.
- Split rate = the share of items with Δ ≠ 0.
- RG is always printed with two baselines:
  - random clicking ≈ 38 (exact for 7-point);
  - "no-gap noise ≈ X", from simulation.
- The RG band labels are positional, and their cut-points are calibrated so that ≥ 90% of simulated no-gap respondents fall in the lowest band:
  - "Very constant"
  - "Mostly constant"
  - "Often splits"
  - "Splits a lot"
- RG is never framed as hypocrisy.

### 4.5 Axis gap (paired items only)
- `G(a) = K · (mΔ₊ + mΔ₋)/2`, where the keyed Δ is computed over items answered in both frames. G is undefined unless both frames are covered on the paired set.
- An axis gap is **notable** when both hold:
  - `|G| ≥ τ_G(n_paired)`;
  - at least 2 paired items on the axis have keyed Δ with the sign of G.
- `τ_G` is a lookup table over n_paired = 3…10, produced by `just calibrate` and committed. The calibration criteria are:
  - respondent-level false alarm: P(any notable axis | no true gap) ≤ 10%;
  - computed under the copy-rate mixture p ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.7};
  - the respondent model is §4.9.
- The methodology page reports power at true gaps of 1 and 2 scale points, and the direction asymmetry from ceiling effects.
- **Only notable gaps get arrows, typology, or a "biggest gap" mention.**

### 4.6 SC × RG map
A descriptive scatter with positional quadrant labels ("Low confidence · Large gap", and so on). The split lines are SC = 50 and the RG "Often splits" cut. Points within the SC band of 50 are drawn as straddling.

### 4.7 Careless/random-responding flag
- **Index:** a within-frame keyed contradiction index. It is the mean, across axes, of the dispersion of keyed values. It is length-normalised, with the threshold calibrated per realised n.
- **Calibration targets:**
  - flags ≥ 80% of simulated uniform-random clickers in every mode;
  - flags ≤ 5% of simulated honest respondents.
- **When it fires:**
  - the hero shows "No reliable match: your answers were too inconsistent to place";
  - the gaps say "may reflect fast answering";
  - a bit in the payload records it.

### 4.8 Matching
- **Coverage gate:** a frame's matches show only if at least 8 of 12 axes are covered and every group has at least 2 covered. Otherwise: "Not enough answers to match".
- **Distance:** `d = sqrt( Σ_cov w̃_a (U_a − A_a)² / Σ_cov w̃_a ) / 200`, where `w̃` are the normalised weights.
- **Labels:** "very close", "close", "loosely similar", or "no close match". The thresholds are calibrated as percentiles of d for respondents simulated *from each archetype's own median sheet* with honest noise (§4.9):
  - very close ≤ 50th percentile;
  - close ≤ 80th;
  - loosely ≤ 95th.
  
  "Close" therefore means "as close as a typical sincere adherent". The careless flag overrides the labels. Moderate and centrist archetypes are allowed.
- **Headline = the fit set** (2026-10-31, replaces argmin naming; full rule and decisions in `docs/method/fit-set.md`):
  - Per frame F (ideal answers U_I, here-and-now answers U_N, both compared with each ideology's *ideal* profile), X **fits** F when no core commitment rules X out and d(U_F, X.ideal) ≤ the "loosely" cut for the test length. A sensitive X needs the stricter "very close" cut.
  - Ideal fit set empty → **"No close match"**, with the nearest not-ruled-out ideology shown as a measurement.
  - One fit → named. Several → all named, nearest first, "this test can't tell them apart"; past 3, "A, B, C and N more".
  - **Split** ("Ideal: A · Here and now: B") only when both fit sets are non-empty and disjoint. Otherwise **constant**, and the page says what here and now fits when it differs, or that it fits nothing. *Changed 2026-10-31 (fit-set review S1):* an empty here-and-now set used to count as a split, but the cut is the 95th percentile, so noise alone gave gap-free respondents a false split 5.5% / 2.1% / 5.9% of the time. See `docs/method/fit-set.md`, decision 3 and open question 5.
  - Distance, the label bands and stability are printed under the verdict as labelled measurements; none of them picks a winner inside the set. μ is still calibrated and published, but no longer gates anything.
- **Joint match (secondary):** the 24-d distance to (ideal, now). Copy: "Your theory-and-practice pattern resembles …". It carries a reference-context caveat, which is stronger when SC < 50.
- **Stability:**
  - 200 draws of U + N(0, SE) per axis;
  - seeded from a hash of the canonical payload bytes, so the owner and every viewer get identical labels;
  - top-1 in ≥ 70% of draws = "stable"; 40–70% = "fairly stable"; otherwise "toss-up". Reported for each frame's nearest as a measurement only.
- **Explanations:** the top 3 per frame, with a "why" line: the 3 axes contributing most to closeness, and the 1 contributing most to distance.

### 4.9 Simulation model (for all calibrations; `scripts/calibrate.ts`, seeded, committed output `src/data/calibration.json`)

**Respondent types:**
- **(a) Latent:** per-axis θ ~ U[−2.5, 2.5]. Each item's true keyed value = clip(θ + item offset ~ N(0, 0.6)), then N(0, 0.8) response noise, rounded and clipped to ±3.
- **(b) Archetype-anchored:** true values come from an archetype's median sheet; the noise is the same as (a).
- **(c) Uniform random clicker.**

**Paired frames:**
- **No-gap:** true N = I. With probability p, the Now answer copies the Ideal cell; otherwise the Now answer is redrawn with independent noise.
- **Gap:** true keyed shift of +1 or +2 on every item of 1–3 random axes.

**Passes:** at 0%, 10% and 20%, random.

**Outputs:**
- τ_G per n;
- μ;
- the RG band cuts;
- careless thresholds;
- label percentiles;
- power, PPV and false-alarm tables, rendered on the methodology page.

**Tests** re-run a smaller simulation and assert that the committed thresholds still meet their criteria.

## 5. Results page (top to bottom)

1. **Hero.**
   - The fit set: "Ideal: A · Here and now: B", "Constant: A" (or "A and B: this test can't tell them apart"), or "No close match".
   - Measured under it: the nearest ideology's distance and band beside the "fits" cut, and its stability.
   - Reality Gap with both baselines.
   - Coverage ("from 68/72 answers").
   - Quick mode shows a "low precision" chip.
   - **When the hero is "Constant", a secondary hook:** the strongest notable gap with its type ("Constant overall · Partway toward ideal on Economy"), or "Very constant: your answers barely change with context".
2. **The 12×2 chart.** 4 groups × 3 rows. Each row has:
   - ◆ Ideal and ● Now, with whiskers;
   - the paired-G line;
   - □ status quo, if given;
   - an arrow only on notable gaps;
   - greyed "not enough answers" rows.
3. **Gaps.** Notable gaps with their typology, "Your reasons", the SC × RG map, and WGI context if a country was given.
4. **Matches.** Top 3 for Ideal, top 3 for Here and now, and the joint match. Each has a summary, a why-line and stability.
5. **Your biggest splits.** The 5 items with the largest |Δ| (own device only, never shared).
6. **Diagnostics and coverage.** Collapsed by default.
7. **Actions.** Share / compare / export JSON / retake / clear data.

## 6. Details

### 6.1 Share link
- **Format:** `#r=` + base64url(payload) + a 1-byte checksum + a terminator `.z`.
- **Payload fields:**
  - format version and **method/catalogue version**;
  - flags: mode, input mode, careless, sliders/SC/country present;
  - 12 I scores and 12 N scores (0…200, 255 = null);
  - 12 + 12 half-bands (0…100);
  - 12 paired G values (0…200, 255 = undefined);
  - notable mask (2 bytes);
  - optional: 12 Q values and typology codes; SC; RG; country (2 bytes).
- **Scores only, never answers.**
- **Optional fields are excluded by default.** Country, SC and status quo each need an explicit toggle. Combining country with SC or Q shows a one-line warning.
- The owner's own results are **computed from the quantised payload**, the same code path the viewer uses. An e2e test asserts the owner and shared views are identical.
- The results route keeps the fragment **out of the address bar** until Share/Copy is pressed, and then uses `replaceState`. Old versions decode against **frozen catalogues** (`src/data/catalogues/v<N>.ts`); slugs are never deleted.
- The decoder validates and reports "link looks truncated" or "made with a newer version".

### 6.2 Shared view and the viral loop
- Opening `#r=` renders the shared result (no item-level sections) and stashes the payload in sessionStorage and app state.
- CTA: "Take it yourself and compare".
- After the viewer finishes, results auto-offer "Compare with the person who shared". If the viewer already has a saved result, "Compare now" is offered immediately.

### 6.3 Compare
- Compare links take the form `#c=<mine>~<theirs>`, built from two payloads.
- The overlay shows both people's ◆ and ● per row, the per-frame distance, and the most agree/disagree axes.
- The other person is labelled "Them". The Compare card defaults to Minimal and reminds the user that it shows someone else's results.

### 6.4 Share cards (Canvas 2D, client-side)
- Sizes: 1080×1350, 1080×1920 and 1200×630.
- Content: wordmark, hero, compact 12 dumbbells, RG with baseline, and the short URL. SC/country/Q appear only if toggled.
- A Minimal toggle hides numbers.
- Blobs are pre-rendered when the share sheet opens. `navigator.share({files})` is then called synchronously on tap (an iOS activation rule).
- Fallbacks: download PNG, copy link. Intent links (X, Bluesky, Reddit, WhatsApp) use `encodeURIComponent` on the whole URL, with a test.
- Share text templates; hero labels pass the sensitive-archetype policy.

### 6.5 Gap typology (notable gap + Q given; positional names)
Let ε = 15, and let I, N, Q be scores on axis a. The procedure is evaluated in order, and the first match wins:
1. If the I or N band crosses Q in a way that changes the order → **Unclear**.
2. If |Q − I| < ε → **Away from a settled ideal** (your country already sits near your ideal, but here and now you'd move off it).
3. If |N − Q| < ε → **At the status quo**.
4. If N lies between Q and I → **Partway toward ideal**.
5. If N lies beyond I, on the far side from Q → **Beyond ideal**.
6. Otherwise (N is on the opposite side of Q from I) → **Opposite direction**.

Non-notable axes with |Q − I| < ε and |N − I| < ε get an informational tag, **"Settled"**. An exhaustive property test confirms that every (Q, I, N, bands) input yields exactly one type. Each type has a neutral one-sentence explanation and canonical examples from more than one tradition (research 04 §3).

--- SUMMARY ---

- **Core:** each of 120 original statements (12 axes, 5:5 keyed) is answered on a 7×7 grid, **Ideal system** × **Here and now**. The question is agentless, and both frames judge the identical statement. The ideal stipulates fallible people, not "no ruler", and a realistic rest of the world.
- **Pass ≠ neutral:** pass both or one frame. Coverage is reported. Thin axes are greyed out, not shown as 50.
- **Scoring:** a balanced keyed mean per axis per frame (acquiescence-proof even with passes). Within-person SE bands. Gaps are computed from paired items only.
- **Calibration:** every threshold comes from one committed, seeded simulation, with power tables published. That covers the notable-gap τ_G per item count, the headline-split margin μ, the Reality Gap bands, the careless flag and the match labels.
- **Non-linearity:** archetypes are scored from role-played answer sheets on the user metric, with ideal + transitional vectors, 3 coders from at least 2 model families, and published spreads. The optional status-quo sliders give an ordered, total gap typology relative to the user's own ideal. Reason chips capture the user's stated reasons.
- **Results:** "Ideal: A · Here and now: B", or "Constant: A" plus a secondary gap hook. 12×2 dumbbells with bands. Top-3 matches with stability and why-lines. System Confidence (now after the items, ideology-neutral, balanced) with WGI context on results only.
- **Sharing and privacy:** fragment-only versioned payload with frozen catalogues, so the owner and the viewer see an identical render. Opt-in preview stubs (200 app-shell copies with per-pair OG). A compare loop for friends. Pre-rendered share cards. CSP only in the build, plus a Playwright same-origin network test. Optional fields are off by default.
- **Open:** pole renames, the "Reality Gap" name, the domain, licences, and the preview-link disclosure.
