# 12 Axes² — Design (v1)

Status: design, 2026-10-31. Inputs: `docs/research/` (01–09 plus critic addenda). Calls marked **[my call]** are the agent's decisions, not Max's. Max can reverse them.

## 1. Intent

12 Axes² ("twelve axes squared", slug `12xx2`) is a political values quiz. It keeps the 12-axis model of the original 12 Axes test but **answers every statement twice, on one grid**:

- **Ideal system**: your own ideal, already up and running.
- **Here and now**: your actual country, today, run by the people who actually run it.

Why: single-frame quizzes mix up *what someone thinks is just* with *what they think their situation requires*. Take a Dane and an Iranian with identical values. A single-frame quiz scores them differently on redistribution, because Iran's elite wealth can't be fixed within its legal framework and Denmark's mostly can. 12 Axes² measures the two separately, then reports how and why they differ.

The ideal is **ideology-relative**. Each respondent imagines *their own* working system: a theocrat, a socialist and an anarcho-capitalist each picture something different. So the relationship between Ideal and Now is not a simple offset. The design handles this in three ways:
- it scores both frames on the same scale;
- it matches both against ideology profiles that themselves have an ideal and a transitional programme;
- it optionally places the respondent's perceived status quo on each axis, so the *direction* of each gap can be typed relative to the respondent's own ideal (§6.5).

### Goals
1. **Accurate and defensible.** Every number shown has a published formula, a disclosed baseline or uncertainty band, and no hidden tuning. It fixes the known flaws of both upstream quizzes (research 01, 02, and `docs/criticisms.md`).
2. **Fun and shareable.** It produces a compact identity label ("In theory: A · Here and now: B"), a striking visual (12 dumbbells), share cards and a compare-with-a-friend view.
3. **Private by construction.** Static SPA, all computation on the device, no analytics, no third-party requests. Results travel only in the URL `#fragment`.

### Non-goals
- No backend, accounts, telemetry, ads or paywall.
- No matching to living politicians, parties or countries. Those caused the upstream's virality and also its biggest defensibility and defamation problems (research 02, 05).
- No population percentiles: we have no norm data (research 09).
- Not a validated psychometric instrument. We say so plainly.

### Anti-goals (failure even if it "works")
- A label that is mostly noise. The upstream's random answerers get 87–93% "compatibility".
- Pass/unsure counted as neutral.
- Framing a gap between Ideal and Now as hypocrisy or inconsistency. Second-best theory says moving toward the ideal on one dimension can make things worse.
- Copying any wording or vectors from 12axes.vercel.app, which is all rights reserved.

## 2. Content model

### 2.1 Axes **[my call: keep the 12 MIT concepts, tighten each to one construct, group as 4×3]**

Scores run −100 … +100. **Negative = left-named pole, positive = right-named pole.** Pole order follows the MIT original.

| Group | id | Left pole (−) | Right pole (+) | Construct (one thing only) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Governance | `fed` | Federal | Unitary | Where authority sits: dispersed to regions/localities vs concentrated at the centre |
| Governance | `dem` | Democratic | Autocratic | Who decides: broad participation and contestable power vs concentrated, uncontestable power |
| Governance | `lib` | Liberty | Security | Individual freedoms vs collective safety, order and surveillance when the two conflict |
| Economy | `col` | Collectivize | Privatize | **Ownership** of productive assets and the scale of redistribution |
| Economy | `pla` | Planned | Markets | **Coordination**: deliberate allocation vs prices and competition (independent of who owns) |
| Economy | `glo` | Globalist | Protectionist | Openness of trade, capital and supply chains |
| World | `int` | Internationalist | Nationalist | Allegiance and sovereignty: pooled supranational authority and global obligations vs national primacy |
| World | `mil` | Militarist | Pacifist | Willingness to build and use force abroad |
| World | `mul` | Multicultural | Assimilationist | **How** newcomers integrate: plural identities vs a shared common culture (numbers belong to the scope of `int`) |
| Culture | `rel` | Secular | Religious | Role of religion in **public life and law**, not personal faith. Renamed from "Irreligious" **[my call]** |
| Culture | `pro` | Progressive | Traditional | Change vs continuity in social norms (family, sexuality, gender, custom) |
| Culture | `tec` | Technology | Bioconservative | Embrace vs restraint of transformative technologies (enhancement, genetic engineering, AI, life extension) |

The methodology page shows a crosswalk to the popular vercel naming (e.g. its "Representation" = `dem`). Axes are presented as **correlated facets**, not 12 independent dimensions (research 03 §5).

### 2.2 Frames (respondent-facing text, fixed)

Both frames evaluate **the same proposition**. They differ only in *which system acts*. This fixes the modal asymmetry where "would it ever" was set against "should it now" (research 03 addendum A).

- **Ideal system**: "Your ideal system, already up and running. It is well run and legitimate by your own lights. Its people are ordinary humans, and it still faces real problems: recessions, crime, wars abroad, new technologies, big fortunes. Decisions are made through institutions, not by one all-wise ruler. Other countries are as they realistically could be. *Should it do this?*"
- **Here and now**: "Your country as it actually is today, with its real institutions, laws, leaders and problems. *Should it do this?*"
- **Shared rule** (always shown as a tip): "If a statement describes a situation, assume that situation is happening in the frame you're judging. Most people give the same answer to both on most questions. Only split them when the conditions really change your answer."

**[my call] Hypothetical reading in both frames** (research Q1): conditions in the item stem are assumed to hold. The "doesn't apply here" view is captured by a reason chip (§3.4), not by changing the answer.

**[my call] Rest of the world is not ideal** (research Q2): only your own system is idealised. External threats and trade partners stay realistic.

The "not one all-wise ruler" stipulation follows Max's brief (assume a system good enough not to need a philosopher-king). It is disclosed on the methodology page as a value-laden stipulation that is deliberately silent on *which* institutions, so it doesn't rule out non-democratic institutional ideals.

### 2.3 Items

- **Bank:** 12 axes × 10 items = **120 items**. Per axis, 5 keyed toward each pole (`key: -1 | +1`), at matched extremity.
- **Form:** declarative statements rated on agreement. Each item is a **standing rule** ("Religious leaders should have a formal role in making law") or a **condition → action** ("If a few people come to own most of the country's wealth, it should be redistributed"). Rules:
  - no status-quo-relative words ("more than now", "too much", "currently");
  - no negations ("should not") where an affirmative polar opposite exists;
  - not double-barrelled;
  - no country-specific institutions;
  - **agent-neutral where the axis isn't about the agent**: economic outcomes are phrased so an anarchist can answer them without endorsing a state;
  - polar (`level` 2 = clear, 3 = strong); no "both camps agree" items; no level-4 absurdities;
  - every item must make sense in **both** frames.
- **Original wording only.** MIT-derived concepts are allowed with attribution. Nothing from the vercel bank. Before shipping, a script checks every item for overlap against both upstream banks (token-overlap ≥ 0.5 → rewrite).
- Each item has an optional `note` (a one-line clarification of terms) and `tags`.
- **Lengths, nested and fixed** (no random selection, so results are comparable):

  | Mode | Items/axis | Total | Keying |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Quick | 4 | 48 | 2:2 |
  | Standard | 6 | 72 | 3:3 |
  | Full | 10 | 120 | 5:5 |

  Quick ⊂ Standard ⊂ Full. Order is a fixed, deterministic interleave: axes round-robin, keying alternates, and no two consecutive items share an axis.

### 2.4 Context block (before the items, ~1 min) **[my call]**

1. **Country (optional, select).** Used only for display ("judging from 🇦🇺 Australia") and to show **World Bank WGI** context (rule-of-law and control-of-corruption percentile ranks, CC BY 4.0) next to System Confidence, as an anchoring aid. Never used in scoring. Omitted from share cards by default.
2. **System Confidence (SC):** 5 items, Here-and-now only, same 5-point scale + Pass:
   1. Courts here treat powerful people and ordinary people the same.
   2. Officials here mostly do their jobs without needing bribes or connections.
   3. When something is seriously wrong here, it can be fixed through legal channels (courts, elections, the press).
   4. Elections here can remove the people in charge.
   5. The people who govern here have a right to rule that most people living under them could accept.

   SC = mean of answered items (keyed positive) → 0–100. Requires ≥3 answered, else "not enough answers". Exit propensity is not asked in v1 (research 04 addendum F: it is a preference, not a perception).

### 2.5 Status-quo sliders (after the items, optional) **[my call]**

"Where is your country now on each axis?" 12 sliders (−100…+100, with a "skip" per axis), shown with pole names and one-line definitions. Unlocks the gap typology (§6.5). Skipping the whole step is one tap.

### 2.6 Ideology archetypes

- About 40–48 archetypes spanning the space. **No living individuals, parties or countries.** Names are neutral and self-descriptive (no slurs or pejoratives).
- Each archetype has:
  - `ideal[12]`: centre on −100…100;
  - `weight[12]` ∈ {0.5, 1, 1.5}: how relevant each axis is to the ideology (0.5 = the ideology is indifferent or internally divided);
  - optional `now[12]`: transitional programme, *reference context: a present-day developed liberal democracy*; defaults to `ideal` when the ideology has no distinct transitional programme;
  - `family`, a contrastive `summary` ("prioritises X over Y"), `signature` (its typical Ideal/Now pattern), and `sources[]`.
- **Coding protocol** (research Q6): each vector is coded by **3 independent coders** (AI agents working from cited sources; disclosed as such). The published value is the per-axis median. The per-axis spread (max − min) is published too. Axes with spread > 60 are reviewed and either reconciled with a cited rationale or down-weighted to 0.5. The protocol and spreads appear on the methodology page. Vectors are editorial judgements, and the page says so.

## 3. Answer UX

### 3.1 The grid (default ≥ 380 px wide)

A 5×5 grid.
- **x = Ideal system** (columns: Strongly disagree → Strongly agree, left to right).
- **y = Here and now** (rows: Strongly agree at top).
- **Diagonal** (bottom-left → top-right) = "same answer in both", subtly highlighted and labelled once in onboarding.
- **Quadrant hints** in small text: top-right "Yes in any decent system"; bottom-left "No, never"; bottom-right "In principle — not here, not yet"; top-left "Only because things are broken here".
- One tap answers both and advances, after a ~250 ms confirmation flash.
- There is **no preselected cell**. A focus cursor appears only on keyboard focus.
- Keyboard: arrow keys move, Enter answers, `S` = same as ideal (diagonal at the current column), `P` = pass, Backspace = back.

Each frame uses five points: Strongly disagree / Disagree / Neutral / Agree / Strongly agree **[my call: 5 rather than 7 points]**.
- Why: 25 cells is a usable phone grid (5 × ≥56 px). A 7×7 grid is not.
- Cost: it gives up some reliability per item. We compensate with more items per axis and show honest bands.
- The midpoint is labelled "Neutral / torn". It is **not** "depends", because the grid itself is the "depends" channel (research 03 C3).

### 3.2 Two-row mode (default < 380 px, and an accessibility toggle anywhere)

- Row 1 is "Ideal system" (5 pills); row 2 is "Here and now" (5 pills).
- After row 1 is answered, a "Same as ideal" button appears.
- Fixed order: Ideal first. It is the principle, applied second to your country (research 03 §10.3).
- Proper radio groups with labels, so it works with screen readers.

### 3.3 Pass (Max's requirement)

Pass means "I don't understand / don't know / don't want to answer". It is **missing data, off the chart, never neutral**.
- **Pass both:** a prominent button under the grid.
- **Pass one frame:** "Unsure about Ideal" or "Unsure about Here & now" toggles. Toggling one collapses the grid to a single 5-option strip for the other frame.
- Passed frames are excluded from scoring. Results show coverage ("you passed 7 of 72"), and axes with too little data say so (§5.1).

### 3.4 Reason chips (optional, non-blocking)

When |Ideal − Now| ≥ 2 scale points, a slim, dismissible row offers "Why the difference?":
- crisis/emergency here
- not ready yet
- can't trust those in charge
- fixing past wrongs
- a step toward the goal
- others would abuse it
- doesn't apply here
- other

One tap or ignore; it never blocks advancing. These answers are aggregated into a "Your reasons" bar on the results page.

### 3.5 Flow and persistence

1. Landing.
2. Mode select.
3. Short onboarding: 3 cards explaining the frames, the grid and Pass, with a practice item that isn't scored.
4. Context block.
5. Items, with progress, Back, and a quit-and-resume option.
6. Status-quo sliders (optional).
7. Results.

Progress autosaves to `localStorage`, guarded by try/catch. A visible "Clear my data" control is on the results page and in the footer.

## 4. Scoring (published verbatim on the methodology page)

Notation: answer values `x ∈ {−2,−1,0,+1,+2}` (Strongly disagree … Strongly agree), or `null` (pass). Item `i` has axis `a(i)`, key `k_i ∈ {−1,+1}` (+1 = agreeing pushes toward the + pole), and frame `f ∈ {I, N}`.

### 4.1 Axis score
`S_f(a) = 50 · mean_{i ∈ a, x_{i,f} ≠ null}( k_i · x_{i,f} )` ∈ [−100, 100].
- Equal weights. With no calibration data, any weighting would be editorial. Extremity is controlled by item writing (polar items, matched across poles), not by scoring weights.
- Balanced keying cancels acquiescence: a respondent who agrees with everything scores 0 on every axis. This is a test invariant.

### 4.2 Uncertainty band
`SE_f(a) = 50 · sqrt( (s² + s0²) / n )`, where:
- `n` = answered items on that axis in frame `f`;
- `s²` = sample variance of the keyed values;
- `s0 = 1.0` is a prior floor (one scale point) that stops a perfectly consistent respondent getting a zero band.

At n = 4, 6 and 10 with s = 0, the band is ±25, ±20 and ±16 on the −100…100 scale. These widths roughly match research 03 §8.1's reliability budget after rescaling. The band is drawn as a whisker. Intensity labels follow `|S|`:
- < 20: "centre";
- 20–50: "leans";
- 50–80: "strong";
- ≥ 80: "very strong".

A label other than "centre" is used only when the band excludes 0. Otherwise it reads "centre-ish (uncertain)".

### 4.3 Coverage rule
An axis in a frame needs `n ≥ max(2, ceil(0.5 · items_on_axis))` answered items **and** at least one item from each key direction. Otherwise it shows "not enough answers": greyed out, excluded from matching, with the matcher's weight for that axis set to 0.

### 4.4 Reality Gap (headline second number) **[my call: the name]**
Over items answered in both frames: `RG = 100 · mean |x_N − x_I| / 4` ∈ [0, 100].
- Always printed with its baselines: **random clicking ≈ 40**; answering identically = 0.
- Interpretation bands, fixed in advance:
  - < 5: "Same in theory and practice";
  - 5–15: "Mostly constant";
  - 15–30: "Context-sensitive";
  - ≥ 30: "Highly context-sensitive".
- If RG ≥ 30 **and** the carelessness flag fires (§4.7), the result says the gap may partly reflect fast clicking.
- Never phrased as hypocrisy or inconsistency.

### 4.5 Axis gap
`G(a) = S_N(a) − S_I(a)`. An axis gap counts as **notable** only if:
- `|G(a)| ≥ τ_G`;
- coherence `C(a) = |Σ k_i Δ_i| / Σ |Δ_i| ≥ τ_C` (where `Δ_i = x_N − x_I`);
- at least 2 items on the axis have keyed Δ in the same direction as `G`.

The thresholds `τ_G` and `τ_C` are **calibrated by a seeded simulation, committed to the repo and run as a test**. The criterion is that a respondent with no true gap shows a notable gap on ≤ 5% of axes.
- Simulated respondent: true N = I on every item. Each answer is a true value plus noise with SD 0.8 scale points. With probability 0.6 the Now answer copies the Ideal cell (the grid makes the diagonal easy), and otherwise it is independently noisy.
- The ≤ 5% criterion must also hold under a pessimistic variant with no copying (p = 0).

Only notable gaps get arrows, typology, or a "biggest gap" mention. Otherwise the result says "your gaps are spread thinly across axes".

### 4.6 System Confidence × Reality Gap map
A 2-D plot with SC on x and RG on y. Quadrants are descriptive only:
- high SC, low RG: "Constant, confident";
- low SC, high RG: "Adapts to a broken system";
- high SC, high RG: "Gradualist / transitional";
- low SC, low RG: "Constant despite low confidence".

The split lines are SC = 50 and RG = 15, chosen a priori and disclosed. The map is shown as theory-driven description, not as a norm.

### 4.7 Response-style diagnostics (display only, no hidden correction)
- diagonal rate (share of N = I);
- extreme rate;
- midpoint rate;
- pass rate;
- **carelessness flag**: RG ≥ 30 and mean axis coherence < 0.35 among axes with Σ|Δ| ≥ 2.

Each diagnostic shows a gentle caveat when extreme.

### 4.8 Matching
- User vectors `U_I` and `U_N`, axes masked by coverage.
- Distance to archetype `A` in frame `f`: `d = sqrt( Σ w_a (U_a − A_a)² / Σ w_a ) / 200` ∈ [0, 1].
- **Headline pair:**
  - "In theory: argmin_A d(U_I, A.ideal)";
  - "Here and now: argmin_A d(U_N, A.ideal)". This reads as "what you'd push for here resembles B's ideal". It uses the same catalogue and is context-free (research Q5/C7).
- **Joint match (secondary):** the 24-d distance of (U_I, U_N) to (A.ideal, A.now). Shown as "Your theory-plus-practice pattern most resembles …", with the reference-context caveat, which is stronger when SC < 50.
- **Similarity labels**, no "% compatible":
  - d < 0.12: "very close";
  - < 0.20: "close";
  - < 0.30: "loosely similar";
  - otherwise "no close match", in which case the nearest is still shown, honestly labelled.
  - Thresholds are checked by simulation so that a random answerer's top match is usually "loosely similar" or worse. Test assertion: in the median random case, top-1 d ≥ 0.20.
- **Stability:** 200 seeded draws of U perturbed by N(0, SE) per axis. The result is labelled:
  - top-1 in ≥ 70% of draws: "stable";
  - 40–70%: "fairly stable";
  - otherwise "toss-up between A and B".
  - The seed is derived from the answers, so the same answers always give the same output.
- Show the top 3 per frame, each with a "why" line: the 3 axes that contribute most to closeness and the 1 that contributes most to distance.

## 5. Results page

Order of sections, top to bottom:
1. **Hero:** "In theory: A · Here and now: B". Label stability is shown. If A == B: "Same in theory and practice: A". Reality Gap with its baseline. SC, if answered.
2. **The 12×2 chart.** 12 rows grouped 4×3. Each row is a track from left pole to right pole with:
   - ◆ Ideal and ● Now, joined by a line;
   - a whisker for the band;
   - □ status quo, if given;
   - an arrow only on notable gaps;
   - greyed rows for "not enough answers".
3. **Gap insights:** notable gaps with typology (§6.5 if sliders given), "Your reasons" bar, and the SC × RG map.
4. **Matches:** top 3 for In theory, top 3 for Here and now, plus the joint match. Each has a summary, a why-line and the stability label.
5. **Your biggest splits:** the 5 items with the largest |Δ|. These are the statements where your two answers differ most.
6. **Diagnostics and coverage:** answers passed and diagnostic caveats, collapsed by default.
7. **Share / compare / export / retake / clear data.**

## 6. Details

### 6.1 Share link
- `https://<host>/#r=1.<payload>`, where `payload` is base64url of bytes:
  - version;
  - flags (sliders present, country present, SC present, mode);
  - 12 × I scores, 12 × N scores, each quantised to 0…200, with 255 = null;
  - 12 × status quo (optional);
  - SC (optional);
  - RG;
  - country (optional, 2 ASCII bytes);
  - 12 × I and 12 × N half-band widths (0…100, quantised), so a shared view can redraw whiskers and recompute stability.
- About 60–80 characters.
- Scores only, **never answers**. The decoder validates length and ranges and refuses unknown versions with a friendly message.

### 6.2 Shared-result view
- Renders the same results page from the fragment, minus item-level sections and with a "Shared result" banner.
- CTA: "Take it yourself".
- If the viewer has their own result saved locally: "Compare with mine". Matches are recomputed locally from the decoded scores, so the link contains no labels.

### 6.3 Compare view
Two users overlaid. Each row shows both people's ◆ and ● in two colours, plus per-frame distance and the axes where they agree and disagree most. A Compare card is available for sharing.

### 6.4 Share cards (client-side Canvas 2D, no html-to-image dependency)
- Sizes: 1080×1350 (post), 1080×1920 (story), 1200×630 (link/X).
- Content: wordmark, hero pair, the 12 dumbbells (compact), Reality Gap with baseline, SC (optional), short URL. Country is hidden unless toggled.
- A "Minimal" toggle hides the numbers (research 05: people hesitate to post political views).
- `navigator.share({files})` where supported. Fallbacks: download PNG and copy link.
- Share text templates (no emoji spam). Intent links are plain URLs for X, Bluesky, Reddit and WhatsApp; there are no SDKs.

### 6.5 Gap typology (requires status quo Q(a) for the axis, and a notable gap)
With I = S_I, N = S_N and Q = status quo on axis a, and tolerance ε = 15:

| Type | Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Settled** | \|Q−I\| < ε and \|N−I\| < ε | Already where you want it |
| **Holding** | \|N−Q\| < ε and \|I−Q\| ≥ ε | "Not now; stability first" |
| **Reformist** | N strictly between Q and I | Move toward the ideal, less far |
| **Overshoot** | N beyond I, on the far side from Q | Temporary over-correction (rectification) |
| **Counter-move** | N on the opposite side of Q from I | Second-best or transitional reversal (e.g. infant-industry protection, a vanguard state) |

Each type comes with one sentence and a canonical historical example (research 04 §3). This is how the ideology-relative non-linearity becomes readable.

### 6.6 OG / link previews **[my call]**

- The share UI offers a "rich preview link" (shows the ideal-type name in the path, so the host can see it) or a "private link" (root path, fragment only). The trade-off is disclosed in one line.
- Per-pair stubs (≈2,000 images) are out of scope for v1.

### 6.8 Privacy and hosting
- Static build, deployable to GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages, with a configurable base path.

--- SUMMARY ---

- **Core idea:** every 12-axis statement is answered on a 5×5 grid, **Ideal system** (your own ideal, already working; ordinary people, real problems; no philosopher-king) × **Here and now** (your actual country). Both frames judge the identical proposition; conditions in a statement are assumed to hold in both.
- **Pass ≠ neutral:** Pass (both or per frame) is missing data. Axes without enough answers say so instead of showing 50%.
- **Content:** 120 original items (10/axis, 5:5 balanced, polar, agent-neutral, no status-quo-relative wording), in nested fixed lengths of 48/72/120. Also a 5-item System Confidence block, an optional country with World Bank WGI context, and optional status-quo sliders. About 40–48 archetypes with ideal + transitional vectors, each coded by 3 independent coders with spreads published.
- **Scoring:** a transparent equal-weight keyed mean per axis per frame, with uncertainty bands. **Reality Gap** = mean |Now−Ideal| on 0–100, always printed beside the random-clicking baseline (~40).
